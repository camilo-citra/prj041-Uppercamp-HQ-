## **1\. Meeting Objective / Purpose**

To establish core fire safety requirements, structural priorities, and MEP/HVAC routing strategies for the multi-floor phased renovation and roof terrace development.

## **2\. Executive Summary**

The session successfully established the critical design constraints dictated by fire safety and HVAC requirements. Major breakthroughs included identifying the necessity for a continuous secondary staircase to handle the anticipated high occupancy of the roof terrace, and the strategic decision to allocate roof space for HVAC condensers immediately to avoid costly late-stage relocations. Moving forward, the team will prioritize conceptual alignment with the City's Fire Chief before finalizing interior layouts to mitigate compliance risks.

## **3\. Agenda Items & Discussion Notes**

**Agenda Item 1: Roof Terrace & Staircase Requirements**

* The roof terrace will match the footprint of the floors below, but currently lacks a continuous stairwell from the third floor.  
* Due to the 45-meter escape distance rule and anticipated high occupancy (potentially classified as A1 Entertainment, allowing \~200 people), a secondary staircase acting as a safe zone is required.  
* Stairwell width may need to be 1.8m if occupancy exceeds standard limits.

**Agenda Item 2: Lifts & Accessibility (Part M & S Compliance)**

* The existing goods lift is non-compliant. Replacing it with a passenger/stretcher lift requires a 1.5m downward excavation and underpinning, which is complex and costly.  
* Council regulations regarding disability access on upper floors will dictate whether refuge areas are required in the stairwells and if the stretcher lift is mandatory.

**Agenda Item 3: Regulatory Approval & Fire Concept**

* Fire separation lobbies are required on all floors to prevent fire spread.  
* Heritage considerations for the older building will require careful integration of fire safety features (e.g., specialized paints, fire doors that automatically close).  
* Rather than designing in a vacuum, the team agreed to draft a high-level fire concept plan and present it to Benjamin Ellis (City Fire Chief) to secure early buy-in.

**Agenda Item 4: Mechanical & HVAC Planning**

* HVAC condensers must be located on the roof. Fresh air and cooling will be managed floor-by-floor using weather louvers and ducting to avoid the need for large, vertical fire-separated shafts.  
* Space for condensers must be allocated in the current design phase to prevent massive re-commissioning costs later.

## **4\. Decisions Made**

* **Decision 1:** The team will present a high-level conceptual fire and MEP design to the fire chief to negotiate constraints on the existing building prior to finalizing technical architectural drawings.  
* **Decision 2:** Space for air conditioning condensers will be permanently allocated on the roof during this immediate design phase.  
* **Decision 3:** Communication workflows will be standardized to a single email thread/platform for all meeting minutes and CAD file sharing to ensure alignment.  
* **Decision 4:** Coordination meetings will be held weekly during these early project stages to manage the high volume of information.

## **5\. Risks, Issues, & Roadblocks**

* **Risk/Issue:** Lift installation and associated structural excavation (1.5m down) poses significant cost and waterproofing challenges. (Status: Open)
  * **Mitigation/Next Step:** The design team must verify with the council if full accessibility compliance is mandatory for the second and third floors before committing to the lift installation.  
* **Risk/Issue:** High occupancy calculations for the roof terrace could mandate a 1.8m wide staircase, consuming valuable floor space on lower levels. (Status: Open)
  * **Mitigation/Next Step:** Pieter to draft a conceptual layout plotting the longest escape routes, which will be tested with the Fire Chief for alternative compliance paths.  
* **Risk/Issue:** Structural capacity for the new roof terrace and condenser loads is currently unknown. (Status: Open)
  * **Mitigation/Next Step:** Await the structural engineering report (expected Friday) to determine if existing columns require reinforcement.
